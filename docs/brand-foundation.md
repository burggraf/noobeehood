# NooBeehood Brand Foundation

**Status:** Initial working direction

**Brand:** NooBeehood

**Domain:** `noobeehood.com`

**First hive:** Manta + Manabí, Ecuador

## Core idea

**NooBeehood** combines “new neighborhood” with a bee-and-hive metaphor. The brand centers on the **NooBee**: a person finding their way in a new place, learning from the community, and contributing what they know.

> **NooBeehood**
>
> **Your new neighborhood.**

A hive is more than a location. It is a useful local community built one trusted contribution at a time.

## Brand promise

**NooBeehood helps every NooBee find local answers, make connections, and help build a thriving hive.**

## Naming system

- **NooBeehood** — the parent brand and app.
- **NooBee** — the person at the center of the experience.
- **Hive** — a local community and its shared knowledge.
- **Manta + Manabí Hive** — the first hive.
- **Hive building** — contributing answers, corrections, listings, guides, or welcoming activities.
- **Scout** — a NooBee who is “just looking,” exploring whether a place could become their next neighborhood. Like a scout bee searching beyond the hive and returning with useful signals, a Scout gathers local context, compares possibilities, and may share what they learn.

Use plain language for important actions. Bee language should add warmth, not make the app harder to understand. Prefer **Search**, **Guides**, **Homes**, and **Events** over obscure labels such as “Forage” or “Honeycomb.”

## Message hierarchy

### Primary tagline

**Your new neighborhood.**

### Supporting line

**Find your people. Build your hive.**

### Short description

> Local answers, useful guides, trusted connections, and community life for every NooBee finding a home in Manta and Manabí.

### Campaign lines

- Just looking? Start as a Scout.
- Scout the neighborhood before you make the move.
- New here? You belong in the hive.
- Find what you need. Share what you know.
- Every answer helps build the hive.
- A warmer welcome to local life.
- From new arrival to good neighbor.
- Make a beeline for what matters.

Use bee puns sparingly; one memorable line is charming, a paragraph of them is noise.

## Personality

- **Welcoming:** newcomers should feel invited, not tested.
- **Useful:** answer the question quickly and clearly.
- **Neighborly:** sound like a capable local helping over coffee.
- **Energetic:** optimistic and active without becoming childish.
- **Grounded:** reflect real daily life, not an expat fantasy.
- **Collaborative:** the hive improves when people contribute.

## Voice

Write in warm, direct language.

- Say **“Here’s where to start”**, not **“Users may initiate onboarding.”**
- Say **“Last checked on…”**, not **“Verified forever.”**
- Invite contribution with **“Help build this hive”** or **“Share an update.”**
- Explain unfamiliar local terms instead of assuming knowledge.
- Keep important directions literal even when surrounding copy is playful.

## Visual idea

The identity should feel like a modern community app inspired by real bees, honey, wax, woven hive structure, and warm Ecuadorian light—not a cartoon beekeeping brand.

### Logo direction

Build the primary mark around a distinctive **NooBee**:

- a simple bee silhouette or friendly geometric bee;
- two wings that can suggest an **N**;
- a body or surrounding cell that suggests a hexagon;
- a compact form that remains recognizable as an app icon;
- an optional cluster of connected cells for hive-level graphics.

The wordmark should emphasize **NooBee**, with “hood” quieter but still readable as one brand name. Avoid separating the public name into “Noo Bee Hood.”

### Core palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Bee Gold** | Warm golden yellow | `#F6B800` | Primary brand color, key actions, highlights |
| **Honey Amber** | Deep honey orange | `#D97706` | Active states, depth, illustrations |
| **Hive Charcoal** | Soft near-black | `#241F17` | Primary text, bee markings, dark surfaces |
| **Beeswax** | Warm pale yellow | `#FFF1B8` | Cards, selected states, gentle emphasis |
| **Honeycomb Cream** | Warm off-white | `#FFF9E8` | Main background |
| **Pollen White** | Clean warm white | `#FFFEF8` | Raised surfaces and content areas |
| **Meadow Green** | Natural green | `#3F6B45` | Success, local-life accents, vegetation |

### Color usage

- Let **Honeycomb Cream** and **Pollen White** carry most backgrounds.
- Use **Hive Charcoal** for readable text and structural contrast.
- Reserve **Bee Gold** for the brand, calls to action, and moments of delight.
- Use **Honey Amber** to deepen gold areas; do not rely on yellow alone for text or status.
- Use **Meadow Green** sparingly to connect the hive to place, growth, and community health.
- Pair gold buttons with charcoal text. Pair charcoal buttons with cream text.
- Avoid black-and-yellow hazard-striping, neon yellow, muddy browns, or wall-to-wall honey gradients.

### Shapes and patterns

- Use hexagons as a flexible grid, badge, crop, or subtle background texture.
- Prefer partial honeycomb clusters over repeating every surface as a hexagon.
- Rounded corners keep the geometry friendly.
- Thin connecting lines can show contributions becoming shared hive knowledge.
- Bee flight paths may guide attention in illustrations, but never replace navigation cues.

### Imagery

Show real people, local businesses, food, homes, streets, coastline, gatherings, and daily life in Manta and Manabí. Use bee and hive illustration as a framing device, not as a substitute for the place itself.

## Brand guardrails

- Do not make the brand childish, sugary, or mascot-only.
- Do not refer to members as workers, drones, queens, or a swarm.
- Do not overuse honey as a reward metaphor.
- Do not hide standard app actions behind bee terminology.
- Do not make every element yellow or hexagonal.
- Keep the NooBee welcoming to newcomers while respecting established residents and Ecuadorian contributors.
